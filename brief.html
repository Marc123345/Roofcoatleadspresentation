Twenty contractors pay you every month and not one of them is under contract. They can walk on any given morning. They don't. That is the only proof that matters, and most agencies cannot say it.

The ones who stayed

Six of them have been with you four months or more. Talbet since March. Brightshield and Summit since April. Restore America, 5 Star and R4 since May. Nobody stays half a year on a cancel-anytime deal out of politeness.

Talbet is coming up on six months. If he asks whether anyone actually sticks, that is the name to say — a contractor who has had six separate chances to cancel and has not taken one of them.

The month that proves it

In August you signed nine new clients in thirty-one days. Imperviase, Ballman, Steel Guard, International Roofing CT, Commercial Roofing Specialists, Tri State, Truebilt, Restoration Roofing, Build Co. Then Jeffrey Hoopes.

That is nearly half your book signed inside one month. You are not selling something unproven. You are selling the thing nine contractors bought in the time it takes to run one campaign.

The three stories

Ron at Build Co left and came back for round two. People don't do that for a service that didn't work. Use this one when he is worried about being locked in — the door swings both ways and someone walked back through it.

Pete Kelley told you two days in that this was exactly why he'd quit Facebook leads. You held the line. He backed down the same day, stayed, and last week handed you his own business partner. A man who was ready to cancel now sends you his people.

A contractor in Washington told you Facebook leads don't work. Four weeks later he closed two roofs at 20,000 square feet each off your leads. Byron sells restoration on commercial flat roofs. That is his exact job size — say it out loud on this call.

If he says

"I've tried Facebook leads before."

So had Pete Kelley, and he told you so on day two. So had the contractor in Washington, who then closed two 20,000 sq ft roofs. The objection is not new and it is not disqualifying — it is the same sentence the people who stayed longest opened with.

"How do I know you won't disappear after a month?"

Nobody here is on a contract. Six have been paying four months or more anyway. If it stopped working, twenty people could stop paying tomorrow morning and nothing would prevent them.

"Do you work with my competitor?"

Fourteen states, twenty contractors. Ask where he works before you answer — that is slide 9, and the honest answer is usually no.

Where this already works

Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Florida, Texas, Michigan, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Washington, Indiana, Tennessee, Connecticut, Virginia — and Idaho as of yesterday. Fourteen states. If he is in one of them, someone near him is already paying for this. If he is not, he is first.

Go in the right way round

You are the one doing him the favour. He clicked your ad on a Sunday night and booked himself an hour later. He came looking for you.

  • Ask where he works.
  • Ask how many big flat roofs are inside his drive radius.
  • Then stop talking and let him talk himself into it.
